The Floor Lottery:
What's Under Your Carpet?

In the century-home community, "floor lottery" is the most beloved phrase there is — tearing out old carpet and finding original hardwood underneath. Here's how to check before you pull, what restoration costs, and when to stop.

The phenomenon

Walk into any r/centuryhomes thread and you'll find someone who just peeled back wall-to-wall carpet to reveal original oak or pine floors. It's the highest-upvoted category of renovation reveals for one reason: it's a 1940s house's beauty secret, free for the price of a weekend.

Check the ticket before you pull

Five minutes of inspection tells you whether the gamble is worth it:

  • Lift a corner at a doorway or vent. The best windows into the subfloor.
  • Press the carpet pile. Firm underneath usually means wood; soft and springy can mean particle board or plywood.
  • Sniff. A sweet, musty odor means moisture damage that may have ruined the wood below.
  • Check nail lines. Rows of carpet-strip staples tell you how ready the wood is to clean up.
  • Look at room size. If the layout matches original floor-plan widths (typically 3-4" boards), you're likely on the old surface.

Rule of thumb: a whole-house refinish for 1,800 sq ft runs $3,800 – $7,200 professionally, or about a third of that DIY with rented sanders. That's almost always cheaper than new engineered flooring — and it keeps the house honest.

When to NOT sand

  • The remaining top layer is less than 1/4" — check at a threshold or vent.
  • There's heavy pet urine or oil staining through the thickness.
  • The boards are spongy or you can see daylight through gaps (rot or failed subfloor).
  • The floor was previously drum-sanded flat and is now too thin to touch.
