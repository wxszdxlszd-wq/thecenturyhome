The Bathroom Bill:
Real Costs for a Century Home

Bathrooms are the century-home community's favorite reveal — and the room owners most often underestimate. Here's the honest 2025–26 math, and why "keep the footprint" wins almost every time.

Why bathrooms are different in an old house

A bathroom in a 1920s home sits on plaster-and-lath walls, often over a cast-iron drain stack, with wiring that may predate the room itself. The same remodel that costs $15,000 in a 2005 house quietly runs 40-60% higher here — not because of vanity, but because of what hides in the walls.

The three price bands (1,800 sq ft house, mid-cost region)

Add a 20% surprise reserve on top. The most common extras in century homes: plaster patching behind new fixtures ($400-$1,200), cast-iron drain replacement ($1,500-$4,000), and subfloor repair where the 1910s plumber did their worst ($800-$2,500).

Order matters: systems before surfaces

  • Water first. Fix the leak behind the wall before you tile over it — every time.
  • Wiring and plumbing before plaster. New supply lines and any rewire happen with walls open.
  • Then floors, tile, fixtures, and the punch list. In exactly that sequence.

Keep the footprint

The fastest way to blow a bathroom budget in a century home is moving the toilet or the tub. New plumbing runs mean breaking plaster, more inspections, and a snowball of subfloor work. Owners who keep the original footprint finish 40-60% under the cost of a gut — and the room still feels "of its time."
